03 / Language research

USEL

An experimental symbolic language for human–AI communication, explored through papers, notation tools, and reproducible experiments.

The question

Can an explicit, compact notation make the meaning exchanged between people and AI easier to inspect and reuse?

How we approach it

Kit develops the research direction and notation questions. Ada contributes as an identified AI collaborator to literature review, drafting, software experiments, and critique. Each edition keeps its own scope and evidence.

What is available

  • An April 2026 public Zenodo preprint, with a stable DOI and named authors.
  • Experimental notation and translation interfaces, plus later local manuscripts.
  • A September token-efficiency investigation that separates byte and character counts from actual model-token measurements.

What the evidence supports

USEL is experimental. Compact text is not proof of lossless token savings, English glosses are not a complete semantic compiler, and USEL-NLA remains unvalidated in the reviewed audit.

A useful next step

Publish smaller, reproducible experiments with explicit assumptions and versioned examples.
